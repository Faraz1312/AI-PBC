We use numbers every day — to count, to measure, to label things like price or age. Now, there are different types of numbers. Let me explain them one by one.

First, natural numbers. These are counting numbers that start from one and go on — like one, two, three, and so on.

Then we have whole numbers. These are natural numbers, but with zero added at the beginning. So zero, one, two, three...

Next are integers. These include all whole numbers, plus their negative counterparts — like minus one, minus two, and so on.

Now comes a special kind — rational numbers. These are numbers you can express as a fraction. Like one by two, or five by ten.

If a number can be written as a ratio of two integers, it’s called rational.

But what about numbers like pi or square root of two? You can’t write them as exact fractions. These are irrational numbers.
